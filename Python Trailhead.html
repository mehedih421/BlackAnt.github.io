Why this fizzles out (and how to not let it)

Last time you practiced "from scratch" for a few days and drifted. That almost always means the practice was disconnected from a reason to open the editor again tomorrow. This trail fixes that in three ways: stages are small enough to clear in one sitting, every stage points at a real project instead of another exercise, and your progress is visible so you can see the trail behind you.

Ground rules that actually work

Twenty minutes beats two hours. A short daily session you actually do beats a long one you plan and skip. If you only have ten minutes, open this page and check the next topic's description — that counts.

Don't finish a stage without starting its project. Reading about loops doesn't stick. Writing the guessing game does. Move to "Basecamps" the moment a stage feels done, even if you're shaky.

Expect to get stuck — that's the work. Getting an error, googling it, and fixing it is the actual skill of programming. It's not a sign you're doing it wrong.

Miss a day without penalty. There's no streak to protect here on purpose. Come back and pick up exactly where the trail left off.

If you keep forgetting what you already built

Finishing a project and then forgetting it isn't a coverage gap — it's what happens when nothing forces you to retrieve what you learned. Reading your own old code doesn't rebuild the skill; writing it again from memory does.

Before starting each new project, rebuild your last one from a blank file, no peeking. Ten minutes, no notes. Whatever you can't recall is exactly what to re-read before moving on.

Revisit a finished project a week later and add one feature to it instead of only moving forward — it forces you to re-read and re-understand code that's gone cold.
